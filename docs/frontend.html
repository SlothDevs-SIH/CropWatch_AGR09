Front end: what people see, and why it looks and works this way

The website is a cinematic landing page, then five role-based screens (farmer, taluka officer, district officer, service centre, admin) built around one map and one plot panel. Last updated 4 November 2026.

Design in one sentence. An earthy, soil-and-leaf look (so it reads as farming, not as a generic dark dashboard), a map that flies from Maharashtra down to a single plot, and plain words everywhere, because the people using it are officers and farmers, not analysts.

1. Colour scheme and why

We first built a blue dark theme and then replaced it: blue felt like a generic tech dashboard and did not suit agriculture. The palette now comes from the field itself: dark humus soil, forest and moss greens, ripening wheat, and two warning colours that are used only for meaning.

The four level colours (used the same everywhere)

NormalWatchUrgentCannot see

Rule: never colour alone. Every level also carries its word, so the screen still works for colour-blind users and on a washed-out projector. "Cannot see" is grey on purpose: missing data must never look like "fine".

2. Typography

RoleFontWhy
Display and headingsFraunces (variable serif)Warm, slightly old-fashioned, rural; gives the page a personality that a default sans would not.
Body and interfaceFigtree (variable sans)Clean and readable at small sizes and on phones.

Both are self-hosted through @fontsource-variable, so the site needs no internet for fonts at the demo.

4. The screens and components

Landing page

A cinematic page in landing/: preloader, hero, a Maharashtra map story, a growing-crop (stalk) animation, the problem, the process in four steps, who it is for, and an honesty strip. Motion is reduced automatically for users who ask for less motion, and the custom cursor and effects are switched off on touch screens.

District page (district officer)

The real outlines of Jalna's eight talukas, coloured by how far 2026 is above the usual share, with a ranking list and CSV download.

Taluka map (the main screen)

  • 3D cells: every farmland plot is an extruded square coloured by level, and the camera flies to a plot with a tilted, rotating move.
  • Season replay slider: recolours the whole taluka week by week with a marker at the 25 September order date, so a viewer sees the stress spread. It re-implements the backend rule in JavaScript.
  • Floating panels: the legend and replay controls sit as translucent, blurred cards over the map; the priority list ("visit first") is a side panel on desktop and a slide-up drawer on phones; opening a plot slides in the plot panel. Panels float so the map stays the hero.

Plot panel

One plain sentence, a chart against the normal range, rain strip (labelled context, not a cause), field-check history, photos, a report button, and the before/after slider.

Before/after picture slider

Raw level colours on blocks were hard to read, so we added real satellite pictures. "Compare years" is the default (a typical ordinary year against 2026, same time of year, so normal ripening is removed from the comparison). "Compare dates" appears only when both pictures are at least 60% clear. Pictures are pre-rendered so nothing is computed while presenting. The white square marks the plot.

5. Words on screen

We avoidWe say
"Crop disease detected""Crops are behind a normal season"
"No problem" (when data is missing)"Cannot see"
"AI prediction""Comparison with a normal season"
"Guaranteed early warning""Early flag, to be confirmed by a visit" (and today we do not even claim early)
"We file your claim""A report to attach to your claim"

The Accuracy page sits in the top bar, not buried, because it is our answer to inflated claims. It says plainly that the signal appears after the order date and that nothing is confirmed on the ground.

6. Language, phones and offline

  • English and Marathi switch (lib/i18n.jsx). The Marathi was written without a native speaker and is unreviewed.
  • Phone first for the farmer screen and map drawers; checked at phone width in the browser, not on a real phone.
  • Offline: a service worker caches the shell and a queue stores field checks to send later. The code exists, but the built-in browser pane cannot register a service worker, so offline mode is not verified.

7. What we borrowed and what we wrote ourselves

From the T.R.A.C.E. project we took ideas only: landing then console, attention-based lists, a one-sentence summary on opening, an "uncertain" counter, a before/after swipe, an offline field workflow. We did not copy code, because that repository has no licence file. See docs/TRACE_NOTES.md.
