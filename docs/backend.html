Back end: what runs behind the website, and why we chose it

The back end collects satellite readings, works out which plots are falling behind normal, keeps logins and field checks, and serves everything to the website. Last updated 4 November 2026.

Design in one sentence. A Python pipeline turns satellite readings into result files; a small FastAPI service with a SQLite database adds logins, field checks, photos, reports and orders; the website reads both. There is no big learned model: "normal" is plain statistics from past seasons, which is fast, explainable to a jury, and not a black box.

2. The analysis pipeline

  1. Fetch (fetch_cells.py): Sentinel-2 windows for 2019 to 2026, any area, with tile merging for whole talukas.
  2. Clean and aggregate: mask cloud and shadow with the SCL layer, average into 200 m (boxes) or 300 m (whole talukas) cells, drop cells with fewer than 30 clear pixels, bin into 10-day windows from 1 June.
  3. Mask to farmland with ESA WorldCover (at least 50% cropland).
  4. Learn "normal" (analysis.py): from the other seasons, aligned by crop age (green-up at NDVI 0.30) so late sowing is not mistaken for stress.
  5. Decide: gap below normal gives Watch (−0.18) or Urgent (−0.36). Too little clear data gives Cannot see. Each plot also gets a one-line reason.
  6. Rank (build_results.py): talukas ranked by excess over their usual flagged share for the date, with at least six past seasons required for a whole area.
  7. Validate (backtest.py): leave-one-year-out backtest, thresholds tuned without 2026, then frozen; compared with a simple "less green than last reading" rule.
Rule we follow: never tune and test on the same data. Thresholds are set from seasons other than 2026. 2026 is the untouched test.

3. The service: roles, safety, endpoints

Five roles

RoleCan do
FarmerSee only their own plots; record field checks; report a problem
Taluka officerSee plots in their own taluka; field checks; reported list; download
District officerSee all eight talukas and the ranking; download
Centre operatorBuild an evidence pack and record a simulated order for a farmer who visits
AdminCounts, create users, read the audit log

Safety measures (tested)

  • Passwords stored hashed; sign-in gives a signed (HMAC), time-limited token. Password rule: 10 or more characters with a letter and a digit.
  • Lockout after five wrong passwords; an audit log of who did what.
  • Wrong role, wrong taluka and wrong farmer are all refused (tests).
  • Photo upload checked by its first bytes and size, not its file name.
  • Security headers (nosniff, no framing, same-origin referrer) and GZip.
  • Production mode refuses to start without CW_SECRET, creates no demo accounts and hides the demo sign-in list.
  • Safe database backups with backup_db.py.

Database tables (SQLite)

users, owners (which farmer owns which plot), checks (stressed / fine / not sure, note, who, when), reports, photos, orders (simulated fee and centre share), audit.

Main endpoints

Whole-taluka data loads lazily (two areas cached), so memory stays about 190 MB, and a taluka map is about 0.2 to 0.3 MB on the wire.

4. The money idea, kept honest

Farmers visit service centres for claim forms. Instead of reselling a government form (which may be free, fee-capped, or restricted), we sell an evidence pack: village, weeks behind normal, rainfall, dates and a satellite picture that supports a claim. In the demo, orders are simulated and clearly labelled "no real money". The pack says it does not file a claim and does not guarantee a payout.
